## Language Conventions

In body text, i.e. prose, an em dash (`—`) is never used. It reads as simplistic AI-generated
language. The goal here is to convey and demonstrate professional, thorough, and well-founded
work.

The deciding factor is whether the dash **structures a sentence or forms a layout**. Within a
running sentence, it structures, and there it is prohibited: a colon, a comma, or a separate
sentence accomplishes the same thing without carrying the telltale style. Outside of a sentence,
however, it can serve as a separator between a keyword and its explanation (`**Term** —
Explanation`), typically in bullet points, tables, and slide lines. There it does not replace
punctuation but sets a column; that is typography, not phrasing.

The test in case of doubt: if a comma or colon could stand in that spot in running text, it is
the prohibited case. If the dash separates two elements that would otherwise stand on separate
lines, it is the permitted case.
